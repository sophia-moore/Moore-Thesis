Holistically Evaluating Reasoning Over Legal Texts in Large Language Models

Sophia Moore
Advisor: Arman Cohan

Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to grow in both capacity and usage, particularly in the legal community. As such, scholars are working to determine whether LLMs can accurately reason over legal texts. A significant outstanding question is whether models can apply the accepted legal framework of Issue-spotting, rule-Recall, rule-Application, and rule-Conclusion (IRAC) to a set of facts.

This thesis modifies an accepted statutory reasoning assessment dataset rooted in American tax law, and it creates a new natural-language subset on which models can be prompted to apply the IRAC framework. Additionally, this thesis presents an empirical evaluation of 19 open-source and commercial LLMs in their modular application of IRAC framework components; provides an analysis of common patterns and accuracy metrics; and outlines directions for future and related work.
